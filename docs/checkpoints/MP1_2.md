- **The memory was not in the app's RSS.** It was 124–138 MB at death, and RSS does not count codec output buffers or HardwareBuffers (dmabuf).
- **The codec log before the 16:00:50 kill**, in the app's own process (`c2.mtk.vp9.decoder`, 1920×1440, 20 fps; Codec2 allocates its output buffers in the app process on Android 16):

  - a new `c2.mtk.vp9.decoder` was created about every 20 s (each cut) right up to the kill (16:00:26, 16:00:47; the kill came at 16:00:50).
- **The two lmkd kills from MP1.1** (12:38 and 12:40) show the same exit-info signature (`LOW_MEMORY`, foreground, RSS 91–94 MB).

### A2. Project 1 played in-process, before the fix (`Mp12Project1Test`)

- **Loading:** Project 1 went through `ProjectManager.loadProject` (read-only) into an in-memory editor. The test host has no save path, so there was no autosave and no Save.
- **The project:** 30 clips, 1078.7 s, composition 1440×1080 at 20 fps.
  - Sources: VP9 at 1920×1440 (the most common), 2560×1440, 1920×1080, 1600×1080, 1440×1080, 1080×1080, 2560×1072 and 1916×1078; AVC at 1440×1080; HEVC at 960×720, 1430×1080 and 1440×1080; AV1 at 1440×1080 and 1920×1080. All are 20 fps.

- **The trace:** `mp1_2/trace_project1_before.csv`, one row at every clip change and every 2 s. - **Each 1440p VP9 decoder holds about 250 MB of dmabuf** (about 37 buffers: the codec commits 31 to the surface, the reader holds the rest). A 1080p one holds about 87 MB. After the pool fills, memory stays flat (slope −0.8 MB PSS per clip, −6 MB dmabuf per clip).
- **Outcome:** the process lived, but playback **stopped at clip 13 of 30** (440.8 s, 448 s wall) with *"Video decoding timed out for this source. Retry or relink the media."*
  - The vendor codec logged `C2MtkBufferManager: fetchNewCcBlock: wait fence ... timed out` for about 12 s first (`mp1_2/logcat_project1_before_clip13.txt`). lmkd was reaping other processes in the same seconds.
  - No onTrimMemory arrived.
- **Project 1 file:** size 9256 and mtime 1795791368111 both before and after the test (in-process check), so unchanged. The adb stat is in Part C.

### A4. Hypotheses (written before the fix was run; marked from the traces)

| # | Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Decoders of earlier clips are not released when the playhead moves on, so memory grows by about one decoder per clip | **Confirmed, with a cap of four.** | `releaseHidden` releases only decoders of hidden or cold-culled sources, and a clip that has ended is neither. Such decoders closed only when a new source needed a slot (4 slots, and the old 96 MB estimate allowed four 1440p decoders). The trace: +250 MB of dmabuf per 1440p clip up to four decoders (1.0 GB), then flat. |
| H2 | ImageReader / HardwareBuffer / VkImage imports leak or are freed late | **Refuted.** | Vulkan images and memories stay at 4–5 over all cuts. Each eviction lowers dmabuf by the size of the decoder it closed, and there is no growth after the pool fills (−6 MB per clip). |
| H3 | FrameCache or the PCM cache exceed their budgets | **Refuted.** | FrameCache is at most 63 MB for 4 decoders (16 MB each, as designed). The PCM cache is a sparse file on disk (50–81 MB allocated), not RAM. |
| H4 | At a cut the next decoder opens while the previous ones are still held, so peak memory is 2+ decoders | **Confirmed (part of H1).** | At every cut after clip 4, four decoders of different sizes were held while the new one opened. Peak dmabuf was 999 MB and MemAvailable fell to 171 MB, with lmkd killing other processes. On Snxw's phone, with Saver on and on battery, the same pressure killed the app (A0). |
| H5 | A native crash in the codec or Vulkan on a specific file | **Refuted.** | There was no signal or tombstone, and the crash buffer was empty in A0 and in every run. |
| H6 | Other | **Seen: a codec starved of output buffers.** | At clip 13 the AVC decoder waited about 12 s for a free output block (`fetchNewCcBlock` fence timeouts) under the same memory pressure. Playback stopped with the decoding timeout. It was re-checked after the fix (Part B). |
