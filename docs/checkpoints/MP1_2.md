### A4. Hypotheses (written before the fix was run; marked from the traces)

| # | Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Decoders of earlier clips are not released when the playhead moves on, so memory grows by about one decoder per clip | **Confirmed, with a cap of four.** | `releaseHidden` releases only decoders of hidden or cold-culled sources, and a clip that has ended is neither. Such decoders closed only when a new source needed a slot (4 slots, and the old 96 MB estimate allowed four 1440p decoders). The trace: +250 MB of dmabuf per 1440p clip up to four decoders (1.0 GB), then flat. |
| H2 | ImageReader / HardwareBuffer / VkImage imports leak or are freed late | **Refuted.** | Vulkan images and memories stay at 4–5 over all cuts. Each eviction lowers dmabuf by the size of the decoder it closed, and there is no growth after the pool fills (−6 MB per clip). |
| H3 | FrameCache or the PCM cache exceed their budgets | **Refuted.** | FrameCache is at most 63 MB for 4 decoders (16 MB each, as designed). The PCM cache is a sparse file on disk (50–81 MB allocated), not RAM. |
| H4 | At a cut the next decoder opens while the previous ones are still held, so peak memory is 2+ decoders | **Confirmed (part of H1).** | At every cut after clip 4, four decoders of different sizes were held while the new one opened. Peak dmabuf was 999 MB and MemAvailable fell to 171 MB, with lmkd killing other processes. On Snxw's phone, with Saver on and on battery, the same pressure killed the app (A0). |
| H5 | A native crash in the codec or Vulkan on a specific file | **Refuted.** | There was no signal or tombstone, and the crash buffer was empty in A0 and in every run. |
| H6 | Other | **Seen: a codec starved of output buffers.** | At clip 13 the AVC decoder waited about 12 s for a free output block (`fetchNewCcBlock` fence timeouts) under the same memory pressure. Playback stopped with the decoding timeout. It was re-checked after the fix (Part B). |

## Part B: fixes (`5a89206`), each from the evidence above

1. **Decoders of clips that are over close at the cut** (H1, H4). This is `DecoderRetention` in `HiddenRelease.kt`, called from `CompositionRenderer.releaseHidden(project, time)` before each frame.
   - A decoder is idle when no visible video layer of its source is active at the frame time or starts within the 0.5 s warm window.
   - **Playing** (the preview sets `renderer.playing`; export always counts as playing): every idle decoder closes at once, before the next clip's decoder opens.
   - **Paused or scrubbing:** the most recently used idle decoder goes to the existing 3 s delayed release, so a scrub back over the cut finds it open. Older idle ones close at once.
   - The 0.5 s warm window and the 3 s hidden release are otherwise unchanged.
2. **Decoders are budgeted by what they hold.** `DecoderKeys.decoderBytes` is w × h × 2 × (cache frames + 2 + 32 codec buffers): about 204 MB for 1920×1440, against the 250 MB measured. The old guess was w × h × 8, 22 MB.
   - The budget is a fifth of the device's RAM (`ActivityManager.MemoryInfo.totalMem`; about 730 MB on the A16).
   - Over the budget the preview leaves the lowest layer out, as it already did over the slot cap, instead of opening a decoder lmkd would punish. Export is unchanged (it rotates decoders).
   - `MAX_DECODERS` stays 4, or 2 on low-RAM devices.
   - On the A16, four 1080p layers still fit; a fourth simultaneous 1440p layer is now left out of the preview.
3. **onTrimMemory** (`TRIM_MEMORY_RUNNING_LOW` or higher, and onLowMemory): every idle decoder closes before the next frame.
4. **H6, a codec that takes input but gives no picture.** This is `DecoderPlan.holdLastPicture` in `VideoDecoder`.
   - Project 1's clip 13 ('lyrfae_1 - VOGUE …', AVC 1440×1080) has 8-byte samples from 16.9 s to the end (about 3000 frames). `c2.mtk.avc.decoder` takes them and outputs nothing; a plain buffer-output decoder stalls the same way (`mp1_2/decoder_probes.txt`).
   - **The rule:** when the codec has taken more than 48 inputs spanning more than 2 s since its last picture, and its input has reached the request, the last picture is held instead of timing out. A held frame is the answer, not an approximation, so a paused preview does not loop re-rendering it.
   - A codec that stops taking input still times out after 5 s. Normal pipelines (VP9 holds about 31 frames) never qualify (JVM tested).
5. **A target before a stream's first frame** (`VideoDecoder.frameAt`, `ScrubFloor.frame`).
   - Clip 17 ('A1 - Video by aeria11st.mkv') has its first video frame at 0.105 s, and the cut asked for 0.003 s.
   - The seek to that first sync frame left the decoder already past the target, so nothing was decoded and the preview stopped with "The decoder returned no video frames". This bug predates MP1.2; it was reached only once clips 13+ played.
   - Now at least one frame is always decoded when nothing is cached.

**Unchanged:** the gesture protocol, the `EditorAction` contract, the batch/draw contract, the evaluator, `SetKeyEasing`, preparation and the document schema (v13). `PreviewController`'s public API is the same: one internal line sets `renderer.playing`. MP2 work was not started (no pre-warming, no parallel decoding, the per-layer flush kept), and there is no quality reduction or resolution scaling.

### Open findings (not fixed in MP1.2)

- **The mid-clip lag Snxw sees:** frames are paced on the median (50 ms at 20 fps, 33 ms at 30), but the tails reach p95 about 61 ms and p99 about 68–87 ms, with 8–46 late frames per run.
  - The memory fix did not change it, so it is not memory pressure.
  - It belongs with the cut hitch to MP2 (decode-ahead and the per-layer flush).
- **What clip 13 shows from 16.9 s:** with the hold it shows its last real picture to the end. Whether other players show more there was not checked.
- **Choppy audio on six Project 1 clips (17, 20, 21, 22, 27 and 28; Snxw, by ear).** These are exactly the files whose audio is HE-AAC declared at 24 kHz ("… - Video by <name>.mkv", Instagram downloads). The decoder outputs 48 kHz (`mp1_2/audio_probe.txt`).
  - **Ruled out:**
    - decode speed: 13–25× real time;
    - format-change order: the change arrives before the first buffer, so no audio is written at the wrong rate;
    - gaps in the samples.
  - Seeking the audio in these MKV files lands at 0–0.13 s regardless of the target (clip 1, AAC 44.1 kHz MKV, too). Each restart of the PCM decoder therefore re-decodes from the start, which makes cold starts slow on short clips.
  - **The cache-vs-decode comparison was inconclusive:** the Opus control (not choppy) also differs block by block, so the method needs level normalisation.
  - **The cause is not established.** It needs its own pass. The two 22.05 kHz HE-AAC "Video by" files (clips 23 and 26) were not reported choppy; that is worth checking by ear.
